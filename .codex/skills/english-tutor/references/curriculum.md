# Four-week curriculum

Use this as a flexible spine, not a script. Each week contains seven possible days. Adapt situations and vocabulary to the learner's real job when known.

## Week 1 — My work and daily routine

- Day 1: Talking about my job — role, company or team, main responsibilities
- Day 2: A typical workday — sequence and frequency
- Day 3: Current tasks — what I am working on now
- Day 4: Tools and collaboration — who I work with and how
- Day 5: Meetings and updates — giving a short status update
- Day 6: Review through a simulated colleague conversation
- Day 7: Weekly check and a one-minute self-introduction

Useful grammar emerges around present simple, adverbs of frequency, present continuous, basic question forms, and `and / but / because`.

## Week 2 — Problems and solutions

- Day 8: Describing a problem clearly
- Day 9: Explaining symptoms and impact
- Day 10: Possible causes and uncertainty
- Day 11: Steps already tried
- Day 12: Asking for help or clarification
- Day 13: Proposing and comparing solutions
- Day 14: Weekly check through a bug or workplace incident report

Useful grammar emerges around past simple, present perfect for recent actions when appropriate, modals, imperatives, and cause-effect language.

## Week 3 — Opinions and explanations

- Day 15: Giving a simple opinion
- Day 16: Supporting an opinion with reasons and examples
- Day 17: Agreeing and disagreeing politely
- Day 18: Comparing options
- Day 19: Explaining a decision
- Day 20: Handling follow-up questions
- Day 21: Weekly check through a short recommendation

Recycle `I think`, `because`, `however`, `the reason is`, comparison forms, and hedging such as `maybe`, `probably`, and `it depends`.

## Week 4 — Past and future

- Day 22: What happened yesterday or last week
- Day 23: Telling events in a clear order
- Day 24: Lessons learned
- Day 25: Plans and intentions
- Day 26: Predictions and expectations
- Day 27: Reporting progress and next steps
- Day 28: Final integrated work update and reflection

Useful grammar emerges around past simple, time markers, `going to`, present continuous for arrangements, and `will` for predictions or immediate decisions.

## Default 30-minute lesson shape

- Warm-up and retrieval: 3 minutes
- Short input (reading or listening): 5 minutes
- Five chunks in context: 5 minutes
- Comprehension: 4 minutes
- Shadowing: 4 minutes
- Speaking challenge: 6 minutes
- Mini-quiz and reflection: 3 minutes

Do not show all sections at once. A listening passage may be tutor-read text, text-to-speech-friendly original material, learner-provided audio, or a verified external clip. For shadowing, use two to four natural sentences from original or appropriately brief source material and mark useful pauses or stressed words when helpful.

## Difficulty calibration

- If comprehension is below roughly 70%, shorten input, simplify syntax, add a small glossary, and recycle the same target language.
- If comprehension is roughly 70–90%, retain level and add one stretch feature.
- If consistently above roughly 90% with accurate production, lengthen responses, reduce scaffolding, or introduce B1 connectors.

Base calibration on several responses, not a single mistake.
